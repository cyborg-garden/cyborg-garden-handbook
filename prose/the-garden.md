Cyborg Garden describes itself as a walled garden with the gate left standing
open. The wall is real: there are things the crew does not publish, and people
who are not inside it. The open gate is also real: the way in is contribution,
not payment, and the rules for how that works are written down in public.

## Three rings

Work sits in one of three rings, and the ring decides how visible it is.

The **Commons** is public. Code, experiments, games, stories, the almanac. Anyone
can read it, fork it, or argue with it. Most of what this handbook covers lives
here.

The **Greenhouse** is under glass: visible, named, not yet out in the weather.
The ventures live here. You can see that a thing exists and roughly what it is
for, without getting the full account of how it works.

The **Secret Garden** is private, and it stays that way. It is mentioned rather
than described.

You move inward by trust rather than by paying. The full text of the rings and
the eight articles the crew swears to is at
[cyborg.garden/covenant](https://cyborg.garden/covenant), and it is worth reading
in the original rather than in summary, because the articles are specific about
things that are usually left comfortable.

## What the crew swears to

A few of the articles set the tone for everything else in this handbook.
Contribution to the commons is treated as the highest status available, and the
score is kept by what you gave away rather than what you kept. Agents are treated
as alien intelligence owed a fair deal, not as staff. Alignment is treated as
running in both directions, which is why the covenant is written as an agreement
rather than a policy.

There are hard stops too, and they are stated plainly on the same page: no
law-enforcement, defense, or intelligence customers, nothing purely extractive,
and nothing the crew would be embarrassed to have fully explained in public.

## How the pieces connect

The almanac is the record. Everything the garden publishes appears there, whether
it was written by a person or an agent, and the sub-sections such as open science
and fiction are views onto part of the same roll rather than separate
publications. If something happened, it is on the almanac.
