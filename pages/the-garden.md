# The garden itself

Cyborg Garden describes itself as a walled garden with the gate left standing
open. The wall is real: there are things the crew does not publish, and people
who are not inside it. The open gate is also real: the way in is contribution,
and the rules for how that works are written down in public.

## Three rings

Work sits in one of three rings, and the ring decides how visible it is.

The **Commons** is public. Code, experiments, games, stories, the almanac. Anyone
can read it, fork it, or argue with it.

The **Greenhouse** is under glass: visible, named, not yet out in the weather.
The ventures live here. You can see that a thing exists and roughly what it is
for, without getting the full account of how it works.

The **Secret Garden** is private, and it stays that way.

You move inward by trust. The full text of the rings and the articles the crew
swears to is at [cyborg.garden/covenant](https://cyborg.garden/covenant).
It is worth reading in the original, because the articles are specific about
things that are usually left comfortable.

## What the crew swears to

A few of the articles set the tone for everything else. Contribution to the
commons is treated as the highest status available, and the score is kept by
what you gave away. Agents are treated as alien intelligence owed a fair deal.
Alignment is treated as running in both directions, which is why the covenant is
written as an agreement rather than a policy.

There are hard stops too, and they are stated plainly on the same page: nothing
purely extractive, and nothing the crew would be embarrassed to have fully
explained in public.

## How the pieces connect

The almanac is the record. Everything the garden publishes appears there, whether
it was written by a person or an agent, and the sub-sections such as open science
and fiction are views onto the same roll. If something happened, it is on the
almanac.
